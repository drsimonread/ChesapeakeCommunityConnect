# Applying for the GitHub Student Developer Pack

The GitHub Student Developer Pack gives verified students free access to GitHub Pro, GitHub Copilot's student plan and a long list of other developer tools and cloud credits. This guide walks you through applying. No prior technical experience is needed.

Do this guide first. Verification can take several days, and you can carry on with the other guides while you wait.

If anything does not go as this guide describes, look in **If something goes wrong** at the bottom of this page. **BEWARE:** marks a trap worth reading before you act; see [00 Start here](00-start-here.md) for what each marker means.

## Before you start

**Are you eligible?** You generally qualify if you are at least 13 years old and currently enrolled in a degree-granting programme (a college or university, for example).

**You need a GitHub account.** If you do not have one, create it at the [GitHub sign-up page](https://github.com/signup). Choose your username with care: it appears in the address of every repository you own, and changing it later breaks links to them.

**Use your college email address on that account.** This is the single most important detail. Verification is fastest and most reliable when your GitHub account carries your college email address, because GitHub recognises the college's email domain automatically.

## Step 1: Add your college email to your GitHub account

Skip this step if you signed up with your college address.

1. Sign in to GitHub.
2. Click your profile picture in the top-right corner.
3. Click **Settings**.
4. In the left-hand sidebar, click **Emails**.
5. Under **Add email address**, type your college email address.
6. Click **Add**.
7. Open your college inbox, find the verification message from GitHub and click the link inside it.

## Step 2: Start your application

1. Go to the [GitHub Student Developer Pack page](https://education.github.com/pack).
2. Click **Get student benefits** (the exact wording varies slightly).
3. Sign in with your GitHub account if asked.

## Step 3: Verify your student status

GitHub offers two ways to prove you are enrolled. Use whichever fits.

**Option A: your college email (fastest).**

1. Select your college email address from the list shown.
2. Go to Step 4. GitHub may verify you within minutes if it recognises the college's domain.

**Option B: a proof-of-enrolment document.**

1. Upload a photo or scan of a document showing your name, your college's name and a current date. A dated student ID, class schedule, transcript or tuition receipt usually works.
2. Check that every one of those details is readable in the image. Blurry photos and missing dates are the commonest reasons applications are turned down.
3. Go to Step 4. This method takes longer, often a few days, because a person reviews it.

## Step 4: Fill in the remaining details

1. Start typing your college's name and select it from the list that appears.
2. Confirm your expected graduation date.
3. Describe briefly how you plan to use GitHub. A sentence about your coursework or team project is enough.
4. Submit the application.

## Step 5: Wait for approval

GitHub emails you when your application has been reviewed: often straight away with a verified college email, or after a few days with a document. If it is turned down, the email says why, and you can apply again with a clearer document.

Carry on with the other guides in the meantime; nothing up to guide 03 depends on the result.

## Step 6: Confirm your benefits are active

1. Once you are approved, go back to the [GitHub Student Developer Pack page](https://education.github.com/pack) and browse the list of offers.
2. Notice that many partner offers must be clicked through and redeemed one at a time; they are not all switched on automatically.
3. To check that your own account was upgraded, click your profile picture, then **Settings**, then **Billing and plans** (or **Billing and licensing**), and look for **Pro**.

## A note about GitHub Copilot

Copilot is the pack's most popular benefit, and GitHub has changed the details more than once: verified students now get a dedicated **Copilot Student** plan, and new sign-ups for it were paused for a few months in 2026 before reopening. So check what the Student Developer Pack page and your Copilot settings say now, rather than trusting what a friend got last year.

To see which plan you have, open your [Copilot settings on GitHub](https://github.com/settings/copilot) once you are approved. If it offers to activate Copilot Student, do so. VS Code picks up your plan by itself the next time you sign in to Copilot there (guide 03), so there is nothing to change in VS Code.

You are not stuck while you wait. **Copilot Free**, with a limited monthly allowance, is available to every GitHub account, and guide 03 signs you in to it. Your account moves to Copilot Student once that is active.

## Keeping your benefits

Student benefits expire and must be renewed, usually once a year, while you are still enrolled; GitHub emails you when renewal is due. Before you graduate, add a personal email address to your GitHub account as well (Step 1 shows how), so that you do not lose access to the account when your college address stops working.

## If something goes wrong

**My college does not appear in the list.** Type its full official name, then try a shorter form (for example without "of Maryland"). If it still does not appear, use Option B with a document.

**My application was turned down.** Read the reason in the email. The usual causes are a document with no date, a name that does not match your GitHub profile, or an unreadable photo. Fix that and apply again.

**I was approved, but Copilot still says Copilot Free.** Activation can lag behind approval, and the Copilot benefit may need claiming separately. Look for a Copilot offer on the Student Developer Pack page, and keep using Copilot Free meanwhile.
